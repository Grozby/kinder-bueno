1. Executive summary

Precisa Surgical builds Precisa Drill, a compact surgical robot that drills a tunnel of a few millimetres through the skull to reach the inner ear or the skull base, guided by the patient's CT scan. The first indication is cochlear implantation; later ones are skull base tumour biopsies and deep brain electrode placement.

We are looking for a Swiss engineering and clinical research site from 2027 and are evaluating Canton Bern for its surgical technology research and its university hospital.

What we are looking for in Bern
  • Research groups in image-guided surgery and surgical navigation for ENT and neurosurgery.
  • Expertise in surgical robotics and micromechatronic instruments.
  • Partners in hearing research and otological surgery for cochlear implantation studies.
  • Medical 3D printing of patient-specific anatomical models for surgical planning and training.
  • AI for medical image segmentation of temporal bone CT scans.

2. Problem

  • Cochlear implantation needs a large opening of the mastoid bone, takes 2–3 hours and needs highly trained surgeons.
  • The facial nerve lies less than a millimetre from the drilling path; damage is rare but severe.
  • Many hospitals cannot offer the procedure because of the learning curve.

3. Solution and technology

Precisa Drill

  • Robot arm with sub-millimetre accuracy, fixed to the patient's head.
  • Optical tracking and force sensing stop the drill near critical structures.
  • Procedure time target: 45 minutes.

Precisa Plan

  • Automatic segmentation of nerves and inner-ear structures from CT.
  • Trajectory planning with safety margins.
  • 3D-printed temporal bone models for surgeon training.
