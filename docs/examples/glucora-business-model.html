1. Executive summary

Glucora Health develops Glucora One, a wearable patch that estimates interstitial glucose through the skin with a microneedle-free optical and electrochemical sensor, paired with a mobile app that gives AI-based insulin-dosing and meal recommendations. The product targets people with type 1 diabetes and insulin-treated type 2 diabetes who find current continuous glucose monitors (CGM) painful, expensive or hard to interpret.

We plan to open our European headquarters and R&D centre in Switzerland in 2027, and are evaluating Canton Bern as the location. We want to be close to clinical diabetes research, medical engineering, and the Swiss regulatory and reimbursement system.

What we are looking for in Bern
  • Clinical partners for a 300-patient validation study of sensor accuracy (MARD) against reference blood glucose.
  • Research groups in continuous glucose monitoring, closed-loop / artificial pancreas systems and diabetes technology.
  • Expertise in AI for nutrition and carbohydrate estimation from meal photos, and in medical machine learning.
  • Biomedical engineering know-how in wearable biosensors, skin-sensor interfaces and signal processing.
  • Access to lab infrastructure for biocompatibility tests and usability studies.

2. Problem

  • Around 500,000 people in Switzerland live with diabetes; about 40,000 have type 1 diabetes.
  • Current CGMs need a filament under the skin, cost CHF 1,200–2,000 per year, and must be replaced every 10–14 days.
  • Many users get raw glucose curves but no clear advice on what to eat or how much insulin to take. Hypoglycaemia at night is still a major fear for patients and families.

3. Solution and technology

Glucora One patch

  • Reusable optical-electrochemical sensor head, 21-day adhesive patch.
  • Target accuracy: MARD below 10% (current prototype: 11.8% in a 42-patient pilot).
  • Bluetooth Low Energy, open interface to insulin pumps (iCGM-ready).

Glucora Coach app

  • Glucose forecast 30–60 minutes ahead with hypo alerts.
  • Meal photo → carbohydrate estimate → bolus suggestion.
  • Clinician dashboard for remote follow-up; data hosted in Switzerland.

Core IP: two granted patents on the optical sensing method (EP, US), one pending patent on the signal-fusion algorithm.

7. Why we consider Canton Bern

  • A strong cluster in diabetes technology and digital diabetes care, with links to the university hospital.
  • Biomedical engineering research on AI in health and nutrition, medical robotics and cardiovascular engineering.
  • The federal capital: closeness to Swissmedic, the Federal Office of Public Health and insurers' associations.
  • Medtech supplier base and precision manufacturing in the canton; bilingual German/French workforce.
  • Open questions for the Economic Development Agency: available lab space, start-up support, tax conditions, work permits for non-EU engineers, and introductions to research partners.
